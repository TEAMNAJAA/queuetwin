# QueueTwin AI — 3D Hospital Queue Digital Twin

Built during **EvoHacks 2.0** · Theme: Projects for Social Good

**Live demo:** https://queuetwin.vercel.app
**Pitch deck:** https://queuetwin.vercel.app/pitch.html (← → to move, F fullscreen, H hides the progress dots)
Opens with a simulated 09:00 morning rush. Click **🧪 Simulate Test Crowd** to turn the bots off.

## The problem
Long outpatient waits are often caused by how the flow is managed, not only by the number of patients. Seniors and people who come alone suffer most, and hospitals have no safe way to test a change before trying it on real patients.

## What it does
**Staff**
- Live 3D digital twin (Registration → Exam Beds → Pharmacy). Crowded zones turn red; waits over 60 min get a ⚠ badge.
- Fast registration with patient contact **and family/caregiver contacts**.
- AI shift roster + full-day simulation: "do nothing" vs AI plan, showing wait change **and** extra staff-hours.
- **Same-staff optimiser**: re-balances the existing staff between stations per shift window (no extra staff-hours) and reports the simulated change in average wait.
- "Best time to visit" advisor + slider showing the effect when patients follow the advice.
- Fairness check (seniors, wheelchair users, children), staff & shift handover, replay, Thai/English, large text.

**Patients & families**
- QR tracking page on any phone (live via Firebase), read aloud, Request Help button, phone ring when it's their turn.
- Public data masks the surname and hides clinical details.

- **🎬 Cinematic view**: a darker, close-up camera mode with glowing bed rings and floating bed tags, for demos.

## Code structure
| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The whole app in one file: the 3D clinic (Three.js), the queue simulation, the staff optimiser, the registration and tracking screens. |
| `api/agentboxd.js` | Small server function on Vercel. It forwards email requests to Agentboxd, checks the input and limits the rate. The API key stays on the server. |
| `api/_util.js` | Shared helpers for the server function. |
| `pitch.html` | The pitch deck, with the live app embedded on slide 3. |
| `train_eta_sim.py` | Generates simulated clinic days and trains the AI wait predictor. |
| `train_mimic_ed.py` | Runs the real-data check on the open MIMIC-IV-ED Demo. |

How the pieces work together:
1. **Simulation.** `step()` moves the clinic forward in small time steps: patients arrive, queue by urgency, get served and move to the next station.
2. **Comparison.** `simDayCurve()` replays a whole day for "do nothing" and for a plan, on the same random seeds, and the charts read from that.
3. **Optimiser.** `optimizeSameBudget()` tries staff placements with the same head-count and keeps the best one.
4. **Background services.** The browser publishes a masked queue state to Firebase so a phone can follow it by QR code. Emails go through the Vercel function to Agentboxd. Sign-in goes to MojoAuth with OpenID Connect and PKCE.

## Run / deploy
1. Import the repo into Vercel (no build step).
2. Environment variables: `AGENTBOXD_API_KEY`, `AGENTBOXD_INBOX_ID`.
3. Deploy. Cross-device tracking uses the Firebase Realtime Database URL set in `index.html` (`DEFAULT_FB_URL`) or pasted in **☁️ Mobile Cloud Sync**.

## How the numbers are made
- Discrete-event queue simulation; patients arrive on a time-of-day curve and are served by priority (emergency → urgent → normal, then first come first served).
- The comparison chart, the advisor and "hours saved" come from simulating the full day under each scenario (3 random seeds averaged).

## AI wait predictor (trained on simulated visits)
- Training data: 254,081 visits over 2,000 days, all generated by the clinic simulation (reg → exam → pharmacy, priority queues). The hourly arrival pattern comes from the real MIMIC data below. Staff numbers and daily demand are randomised every day.
- When a patient arrives, the model predicts their total wait. It gets the time, urgency, queue length at each stage, patients in service, staff on duty and arrivals in the last 30 min.
- Tested on 400 days it never saw (49,023 visits). Gradient-boosting error is **10.2 min** against **23.9 min** for the standard queue formula (57% lower). R² is 0.96 against 0.72.
- Limit: these numbers are measured on simulated data. Real use needs retraining on a hospital's own logs.
- Reproduce: `python train_eta_sim.py 2000`

## Real-data check
The AI tab has a **REAL DATA** card. It comes from the open MIMIC-IV-ED Demo v2.2: 220 real emergency-department visits from 64 patients at Beth Israel Deaconess, Boston, de-identified.
- Evaluation: 5 folds split by patient, repeated 5 times (25 tests). The model is chosen on the training folds only (nested cross-validation).
- Inputs are only what is known when a patient arrives: hour, weekday, triage acuity, triage vitals, chief complaint, arrival mode and the patient's earlier visits. The model never sees how the visit ended.
- Result: the model's error is 210 min against 214 min for always guessing the median, so only 2% better. It wins 15 of 25 tests. We report this as it is.
- What carries over is the real hourly arrival pattern, which the card shows and the simulated training data uses.
- Reproduce: download `edstays.csv.gz` and `triage.csv.gz` from https://physionet.org/content/mimic-iv-ed-demo/2.2/, unzip them into a folder, then run `python train_mimic_ed.py <folder>`.
- Citation: Johnson, A., Bulgarelli, L., Pollard, T., Celi, L. A., Horng, S., & Mark, R. (2023). MIMIC-IV-ED Demo (version 2.2). PhysioNet. https://doi.org/10.13026/jzz5-vs76

## Why the real-data model gains only 2%, and why the approach still holds
- The only open real dataset is an emergency department. Time in an ER depends on how sick the patient is, test results and waiting for a ward bed. None of that is known at arrival, and none of it is about the queue.
- QueueTwin targets outpatient queues (register → exam → pharmacy). There the wait depends on arrivals against staff on duty, which can be predicted and managed. On simulated outpatient visits the model's error is 57% lower than the queue formula.
- Published evidence: a systematic review of outpatient waiting-time strategies (Australian Health Review, https://doi.org/10.1071/AH16275) concludes that aligning resources with demand, raising operational efficiency and improving processes may reduce outpatient waits. Those are the levers QueueTwin works on.
- QueueTwin itself has not yet been tested in a real hospital.

## Honest limits
- Patients, timings and the morning crowd are **simulated**; results are simulation outcomes, not measured hospital data.
- Privacy masking is privacy-by-design for a prototype, not a certified system.
- The Firebase database runs in test mode for the hackathon.
